Feature 4 · Safe Driving Guide

Ten habits that make you a safer driver from day one.

Every new driver makes mistakes. These tips help you avoid the most common ones on UAE roads, in any car.

Top 10 tips for new drivers

  1. Wear your seatbelt on every tripMake sure every passenger wears one too, including in the back seats.
  2. Put your phone awaySet your music and navigation before you start, then do not touch the phone.
  3. Follow the speed limit signsLimits change between roads and areas. Slow down more in rain, fog or heavy traffic.
  4. Keep a safe distanceLeave a gap of at least a few seconds to the car in front, and more at high speed.
  5. Use your indicators earlySignal before every lane change or turn so others know what you will do.
  6. Check your mirrors and blind spotsLook over your shoulder before changing lanes on busy highways.
  7. Take roundabouts slowlyChoose your lane early and give way to traffic already on the roundabout.
  8. Rest when you feel tiredLong drives at night are risky. Stop for a break if your eyes feel heavy.
  9. Prepare for the heatCheck tyre pressure and coolant, and keep water in the car in summer.
  10. Stay calmDo not race or react to aggressive drivers. Let them go and keep your own pace.

Driving in a sandstorm or fog

Sandstorms and morning fog can cut visibility very quickly in the UAE.

  • Slow down smoothly and turn on your low-beam headlights.
  • Increase your distance from the car in front.
  • Do not stop in a traffic lane. If you must stop, move fully off the road where it is safe.
  • Follow the guidance of police and official traffic messages.

Driving at night

At night it is harder to judge distance and to see people walking. Keep your headlights on, clean your windscreen and avoid looking straight at bright lights coming towards you.

New drivers should practise night driving on familiar roads with an experienced driver before long trips.

Always check the official rules. Road rules and fines can change. Check the latest information from official UAE traffic authorities, such as the RTA or the police, before you drive.
